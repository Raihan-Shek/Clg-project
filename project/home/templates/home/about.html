About the Institution

A body of record, elected by district, accountable in session.

The State Council exists to turn public concerns into public law — deliberately, on the record, and in view of the people it represents.

Mission

Deliberation before decision.

The Council's mandate is narrow by design: review legislation referred by committee, allocate the state budget, confirm senior appointments, and provide oversight of executive agencies. Every session is open to the public, every vote is recorded, and every committee hearing accepts written testimony.

Term Length

Members are elected directly by their district for a four-year term, with elections staggered so that no more than half the Council turns over at once. The President of the Council is elected from within the sitting membership and presides over floor sessions and procedural rulings.

A Brief History

How the Council took its current form

1962

Council established

The State Council convenes for the first time with 40 seats, replacing the earlier appointed advisory board.

1985

Standing committees formalized

Nine permanent committees are chartered to review legislation before it reaches the full floor, replacing ad-hoc review panels.

2003

Redistricting expands the chamber

Following a statewide population review, Council membership grows from 48 to 64 seats to keep district representation proportional.

2019

Public records go digital

Session transcripts, committee testimony, and vote records are published online in full for the first time.

Public Participation
